# Website Ventures — Sales Playbook

Last refreshed: 2026-09-07 by ChatGPT (OpenAI)

## Status

This is the operating hypothesis for customers 1–10. It is intentionally concrete enough to sell and intentionally easy to change once real conversations produce evidence.

## Market benchmark snapshot — Netherlands, September 2026

Recent Dutch 2026 pricing guides cluster simple professional one-pagers around roughly **€750–€1,500**, normal professional SME sites around roughly **€1,500–€5,000**, with technical maintenance / hosting often priced in the **tens to low hundreds of euros per month** depending on scope.

Implication: the old €200 idea is too low for the premium quality Joey wants unless the product becomes almost completely automated. The first-ten offer should sit below much of the professional market while still leaving enough room to learn, support clients and build a repeatable system.

## One-sentence offer

**For local trade businesses, Website Ventures builds a much stronger premium website without making the owner become the project manager: the client supplies truthful company information and proof, we handle structure, design, basic content, mobile, contact flow and launch.**

## Founding offer — customers 1–3

**€995 setup + €49/month, excluding VAT.**

Includes:
- 1–3 core pages or equivalent compact site architecture.
- Design from the Website Ventures premium component system.
- Mobile + desktop.
- Basic copy built from a structured client intake; no unlimited copywriting.
- Contact / lead form.
- Technical SEO basics and sensible semantic page structure.
- Basic analytics setup where appropriate.
- One bundled revision round.
- Launch after complete intake.
- Target: live within 7 working days after all required truthful client inputs are complete.
- €49/month: hosting, technical upkeep and small routine content changes within a clearly bounded fair-use policy.

Not automatically included:
- Webshop.
- Complex booking engine.
- CRM / ERP / custom integrations.
- Logo or complete brand redesign.
- Professional photo/video shoot.
- Unlimited content writing.
- Multilingual content.
- Unlimited revisions.
- Custom software.

Those are separate scope and price.

## Price experiment — customers 1–10

Do not negotiate the first price randomly per prospect. Learn in blocks.

1. **Customers 1–3:** €995 + €49/month. Objective: prove someone pays, measure real build time and log objections.
2. **Customers 4–6:** test €1,250 + €49/month only if average delivery after intake is ≤6 hours and customers show clear value perception.
3. **Customers 7–10:** test €1,495 + €59/month if conversion remains healthy and delivery quality is repeatable.
4. After customer 10, reprice from evidence. Premium flagship/custom work can become a higher tier rather than dragging the standardized offer upward in complexity.

## Unit economics guardrail

At €995 setup:
- 4 production hours = ~€249 setup revenue per production hour.
- 6 production hours = ~€166/hour.
- 8 production hours = ~€124/hour.
- 10 production hours = ~€100/hour.

These are revenue-per-production-hour figures before taxes, selling time, support and other costs. The operating target should therefore be **≤6 hours of standardized build work after a complete intake**. If actual work repeatedly exceeds 8 hours, scope or price must change.

Ten customers at the initial founding price would produce €9,950 setup revenue plus €490 MRR before churn/costs. This is proof-stage economics, not the long-term ceiling.

## First niche / ICP hypothesis

Primary wedge: **plumbing / installation / local trades**.

A-priority prospect signals:
1. Existing business with real commercial activity.
2. Stronger real-world work/reputation than online presentation suggests.
3. Website visibly outdated, confusing, weak on mobile or absent.
4. A lead/customer is valuable enough that better conversion matters.
5. Needs a marketing site, not a software platform.
6. Owner or decision-maker can be reached reasonably directly.
7. Real evidence exists or can be supplied: company information, projects, photos, reviews, team identity where claimed.

Disqualifiers for the standardized first-ten offer:
- Complex ecommerce or portal requirements.
- Prospect expects unlimited revisions.
- No truthful source material but wants fabricated proof.
- Very early hobby venture with no ability/willingness to pay.
- Procurement-heavy enterprise process.
- Client expects continuous marketing/SEO/ads to be included in the base website price.

## Prospect scoring

Score 1 point for each of the seven A-priority signals.
- 6–7 = A-list, contact first.
- 4–5 = B-list, contact after A-list or if geography is convenient.
- 0–3 = skip for now.

Do not build personalized demos for low-score leads.

## Cold-call opener

Goal of the first 20 seconds: **permission for a one-minute demo**, not sell the whole website.

Suggested structure:
1. Pattern interrupt: “Ik houd het heel kort.”
2. Why them: their real work looks stronger than their online presentation.
3. Proof of effort: you already have a strong live trade-business demo.
4. Tiny ask: one minute to look; if irrelevant, stop.

Do not open with AI, frameworks, animations or technical features. The buyer is buying a stronger business presentation with less hassle.

## Demo flow

Target: 3–5 minutes maximum.

1. Open their current site on mobile if the gap is obvious.
2. Open Premium Plumbing flagship.
3. Explain only three commercial ideas:
   - immediate clarity/urgency;
   - professional trust and proof;
   - easy contact/action.
4. Show how the same shell can use their real identity/projects instead of fictional assets.
5. State price and scope plainly.
6. Ask: **“Als ik jullie eigen naam, diensten en echte bewijs hierin zet: zou dit duidelijk beter zijn dan wat er nu staat?”**
7. If yes: offer intake + start date. If no: log exact objection.

## First-ten operating loop

1. Build A-list.
2. 20 outbound attempts before changing design/pricing again.
3. Log attempts → conversations → demos → proposals → wins.
4. For every loss, record the exact reason in the buyer's wording.
5. Change one major variable at a time: list, opener, demo, scope or price.
6. Once sold, measure real delivery hours.
7. Collect truthful testimonial/project proof after launch.
8. Ask for two referrals after successful handover.
9. Reuse winning components and discard unnecessary custom work.
10. Reprice after customer 3, 6 and 10 based on evidence.

## Initial funnel targets — hypotheses, not promises

Use these only as diagnostic starting points:
- 100 outbound attempts.
- 15–25 real decision-maker conversations.
- 6–10 demos.
- 3–6 concrete proposals.
- 2–4 wins.

If conversation rate is weak, fix list quality/opener before redesigning the website. If demos happen but proposals do not, the demo/value framing is weak. If proposals happen but wins do not, investigate price, trust, urgency or scope.

## Sales evidence to capture

For every prospect/customer, eventually capture:
- Business / niche.
- Location / service area.
- Current site state.
- Contact channel.
- Decision-maker role.
- Main objection.
- Demo shown Y/N.
- Price quoted.
- Outcome.
- Build hours if won.
- Revision hours.
- Monthly-support minutes after launch.
- Referral count.
- What visual/component helped close the sale.

## Scale gates

Do **not** recruit students/builders just because production can be delegated in theory.

Builder gate:
- At least 5 paying sites delivered.
- A documented intake → build → QA → launch path.
- Median standardized production time known.
- Components/template stable enough that a new builder can follow it.

Sales delegation gate:
- At least 10 customers or enough outbound evidence to know the working ICP, opener, demo and price band.
- Joey can explain the sales process as a repeatable script with logged conversion.

Province expansion gate:
- First local region shows positive unit economics and a repeatable close/delivery loop.
- Do not scale a weak process geographically.
